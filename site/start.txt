# Naresh, the professional
Are you a recruiter, or otherwise representing a prospective employer? Welcome to you too.
I hope you can forgive the sparse aesthetics of this website.

So who is this mysterious programmer rambling on about himself and his half-baked projects? (I hope 
you'll also forgive my hopeless attempt at humor. Then again, it might be a good way to filter me out,
for better or for worse).

Let's see, where should we start... at the beginning, of course! The VERY beginning? Well, perhaps we'll
save that for another time. Let's look at some academics:

I came to Germany in 2015, spent a year learning German, then enrolled myself in the Fachhochschule Aachen's
Bachelor programme for Phyical Engineering (Physikingenieurwesen). I ended up spending too many hours
in a clean room, tweaking mirrors and lasers, and eventually produced my spectacular first academic work
on lasers. I wrote about how a femtosecond pulsed-laser could be constructed, and I spent many evenings
in that lab, trying to prove my hypothesis. I got, somewhere, but some hopeful successor will have the
privelege of one day firing that baby up.

Not wanting to spend my glory years in a dark basement, wearing goggles and having perpetually bad hair
(no offense, laser scientists). I decided that the Laser Physics life was not for me. I leaned hard
into my love for programming and computers, and decided to jump ship to enroll myself at the Master's
program at the Hochschule Düsseldorf for Electrical Engineering for Automation. As much as I enjoyed
delving into theoretical physics, working with and programming machines and other complex systems
was also quite a treat. I graduated in early 2022.

Needing to fund my endless desire to not starve, I also worked a part-time job at a telecommunications
company. They gave me my very own pet project, which would become my first professional programming job.
With the generous help of my colleagues, I built a relatively stable system to run end-to-end tests
on the telecommunications network. It would select random phone numbers from a pool, register them on
the network, and send audio samples through the call. Then it would measure the audio quality and spit
out test data. As someone who did not yet fully understand why segmentation faults happen in C++ code,
I must say, I'm quite proud of how it turned out.
